Generative models operate on continuous and discrete state spaces. This course develops their mathematical, algorithmic, and empirical foundations, with discrete state modeling as the central focus.

Course logistics: Tuesdays and Thursdays, 8:30-10:00 a.m. in Towne 337. Fall 2026.

Course structure

The semester progresses from mathematical foundations and continuous probability transport through continuous diffusion, discrete diffusion, discrete flow matching, one-step and few-step flow maps, and finally optimal transport and Schrödinger bridges. Derivations are paired with worked numerical examples, implementations, empirical evaluation, and guidance procedures.

Learning objectives

  1. Develop a rigorous understanding of probability, the simplex, continuous-time Markov chains, the master equation, and generative transport.
  2. Derive, analyze, and implement classical generative models, discrete diffusion, discrete flow matching, optimal transport, stochastic control, and discrete Schrödinger bridges.
  3. Apply these frameworks across biological sequences and structures, images, audio, natural language, code, graphs, point clouds, 3D molecular data, probability-simplex representations, and continuous latent representations.
  4. Translate mathematical specifications into implementations and evaluate them with benchmarks and ablations.
  5. Read current research and design, communicate, and defend novel discrete generative modeling strategies.

Prerequisites

Students must have completed CIS 5190, CIS 5200, or ESE 5460.

Grading

10%Participation
30%Two written exams

There is no curve. Final percentages use the traditional scale described in the official syllabus. Grades are posted in Canvas.

Participation

Participation includes contributions to lecture derivations, algorithm-design sessions, and implementation examples. Students must arrive by 8:40 a.m. to be marked present; arrivals beginning at 8:41 a.m. count as absent. Each unexcused absence reduces the final grade by one point, or by 0.5 points when reported at least one week in advance.

Exams

Two closed-book written exams, worth 15% each, emphasize mathematical reasoning, proofs, derivations, stochastic-process analysis, and algorithm design. One reference sheet is allowed.

  • Exam 1: material taught August 25 through September 29, including mathematical foundations, flow matching, continuous diffusion and guidance, masked diffusion, and MDLM.
  • Exam 2: cumulative material taught August 25 through November 12, adding discrete diffusion, discrete flow matching, simplex and Gumbel-softmax flows, multi-objective guidance, and latent, discrete, expanding, and stochastic flow maps.

Group projects

  • Project 1 - Continuous Generative Models (15%): a two-modality comparison of continuous flow matching and diffusion, including guided generation and an ablated methodological innovation transferred across modalities.
  • Project 2 - Discrete Generative Models Workshop (20%): a guided discrete generative model evaluated with appropriate baselines, quantitative measures, guidance-strength analyses, and controlled ablations in ICLR workshop format.
  • Final Project - ICML-Ready Paper (25%): an extension of Project 1, Project 2, or both into a coherent contribution supported by theory, baselines, ablations, multi-seed experiments, guidance analyses, and cross-setting validation.

Every project concludes with a 30-minute group defense: a 10-minute presentation and 20 minutes of questions from the teaching team.

Practice problems

There are no graded problem sets. Ungraded practice problems with full solutions will be released throughout the semester.
